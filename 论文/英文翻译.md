Zinc oxide (ZnO)-based ceramic linear resistors offer abundant raw materials, mature processing, high energy absorption capability, and widely adjustable resistivity.Therefore, they are widely used in power-system neutral grounding, current-limiting voltage dividers, and high-power electronic circuits [1,2].Unlike ZnO varistors that rely on a high nonlinear coefficient (α\alphaα) for overvoltage protection [3,4], ZnO linear resistors must follow Ohm's law (α\alphaα close to 1.00) with stable resistivity and a low temperature coefficient of resistance (TCR) [5,6].However, ZnO is a non-stoichiometric nnn-type wide-bandgap semiconductor, and the electrical transport of polycrystalline ZnO ceramics is controlled by both intragranular point defects and grain boundary barriers [7,8].
