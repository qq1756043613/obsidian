Zinc oxide (ZnO)-based ceramic linear resistors offer abundant raw materials, mature processing, high energy absorption capability, and widely adjustable resistivity.Therefore, they are widely used in power-system neutral grounding, current-limiting voltage dividers, and high-power electronic circuits [1,2].Unlike ZnO varistors that rely on a high nonlinear coefficient (α\alphaα) for overvoltage protection [3,4], ZnO linear resistors must follow Ohm's law (α\alphaα close to 1.00) with stable resistivity and a low temperature coefficient of resistance (TCR) [5,6].However, ZnO is a non-stoichiometric nnn-type wide-bandgap semiconductor, and the electrical transport of polycrystalline ZnO ceramics is controlled by both intragranular point defects and grain boundary barriers [7,8].Acceptor states at grain boundaries easily trap free electrons from ZnO grains, creating a negatively charged layer, a depletion layer, and double Schottky barriers that impede electron transport [9–11].When the grain boundary barrier is high, the grain boundary resistance drops with increasing electric field, resulting in non-ohmic current–voltage (I–VI\text{–}VI–V) characteristics [12,13].Therefore, proper control of both grain conductivity and the grain boundary barrier is the key to achieving stable ohmic characteristics [5,6].

This spinel phase inhibits excessive ZnO grain growth and improves ceramic density [16,17].This spinel phase inhibits excessive ZnO grain growth and improves ceramic density [16,17].In our previous research, we carried out a series of studies on ZMT and related ZnO-based ceramics.In Al-doped systems, reducing atmosphere sintering [18,19] and spark plasma sintering (SPS) [20] greatly increased donor solubility and electron-active defect concentrations, yielding highly conductive ZnO ceramics.In In- or Ga-doped ZMT ceramics, atmosphere control effectively lowered the grain boundary barrier, achieving high-conductivity near-ohmic behavior [21] and low resistivity (0.10–0.41 Ω·cm) with good linearity (α=1.01–1.02\alpha = 1.01\text{–}1.02α=1.01–1.02) [6].Moreover, adding La2O3\mathrm{La_2O_3}La2​O3​ to air-sintered ZMT ceramics caused a transition from non-ohmic to ohmic characteristics (α=1.00\alpha = 1.00α=1.00), but the resistivity also decreased to 3.58–13.18 Ω·cm [5].
The above studies mainly increased donor defect concentrations or lowered grain boundary barriers to achieve high conductivity and good linearity, which suits low-resistivity ZnO ceramics.However, practical applications often require linear resistors to cover a wider resistivity range.Many studies have tailored the microstructure and electrical properties of ZnO-based linear resistance ceramics by adding insulating secondary phases (such as ZnAl2O4\mathrm{ZnAl_2O_4}ZnAl2​O4​ [22] or high-content Al2O3\mathrm{Al_2O_3}Al2​O3​ [23]) or doping with metal oxides including Sm2O3\mathrm{Sm_2O_3}Sm2​O3​ [2], MgO [15], TiO2\mathrm{TiO_2}TiO2​ [16], Fe2O3\mathrm{Fe_2O_3}Fe2​O3​ [24], NiO [25], CaO [26], La2O3\mathrm{La_2O_3}La2​O3​ [27], ZrO2\mathrm{ZrO_2}ZrO2​ [28], and Sc2O3\mathrm{Sc_2O_3}Sc2​O3​ [29].However, existing systems still struggle to combine higher resistivity, excellent I–VI\text{–}VI–V linearity, and a low TCR.
